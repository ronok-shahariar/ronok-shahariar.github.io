My Research & Engineering Journey

From wireless channel theory to intelligent communication systems

A short story of the questions that shaped my research, the engineering challenges I worked on, and the direction I want to explore through graduate study.

01 / Undergraduate Thesis

Wireless fading & channel secrecy

How do fading conditions and eavesdroppers affect the security of a wireless channel?

Dual-hop wireless communication model with randomly distributed eavesdroppers

What I did

I modeled a dual-hop wireless network using the generalized α−µ fading model and studied source-hop, relay-hop, and simultaneous eavesdropping.

What I found

Simultaneous eavesdropping across both hops produced the highest secrecy risk, while stronger legitimate channels and receive diversity improved secure communication.

α−µ Fading Dual-Hop SIMO Physical-Layer Security

02 / Professional Engineering

Satellite RF & high-speed data systems

What happens when wireless communication moves from mathematical models to a real high-speed system?

What I did

At Siliconova, I worked with FPGA-based SDRs and a 100 Gbps-class DPDK data path for capturing large satellite RF datasets, including systems intended for remote deployments.

What I achieved

I contributed to capture, recording, replay, telemetry, and validation workflows for high-rate RF data while maintaining data integrity under sustained workloads.

03 / FPGA & VLSI

Understanding what happens inside the hardware

How is high-speed data kept correct inside the hardware itself?

Example FPGA platform illustrating high-speed digital processing hardware

What I explored

I moved deeper into RTL design, FIFO and FSM logic, RISC-V architecture, and cycle-accurate hardware verification.

What I built

I designed and verified reusable digital blocks and a 3-stage RISC-V pipeline with hazard handling, forwarding, branch control, and automated Cocotb regression testing.

04 / Future Research

Intelligent RF signal analysis

Can a communication system not only capture RF signals, but also understand what is happening inside them?

What I want to investigate

I want to combine real RF measurements, wireless channel analysis, AI-based anomaly detection, and FPGA acceleration to identify unusual signal behavior and changing fading conditions.

Why it matters

This could move intelligent analysis closer to the hardware and help high-speed communication systems detect important signal behavior more efficiently.

My goal is to pursue an M.Sc. and eventually a Ph.D. to develop this research direction more deeply.
