01 / Undergraduate Research

Where my interest in wireless communication began

Wireless Communication & Channel Secrecy

Can a generalized fading model provide a common way to study different wireless channel conditions?

That question led me to explore wireless channel modeling and physical-layer security during my undergraduate thesis. Because wireless signals are broadcast through an open medium, unintended receivers can also experience the channel, making secrecy an important part of system design. I studied a dual-hop wireless network with randomly distributed eavesdroppers using the α−µ generalized fading model and analyzed attacks at the source, relay, and both hops. The results showed that simultaneous eavesdropping across both hops creates the most critical secrecy condition, while stronger legitimate-channel conditions and receive diversity improve secure communication.

02 / Professional Engineering

From wireless theory to a real high-speed system

Satellite Data Acquisition & High-Speed RF Systems

What happens when wireless communication leaves the equations and becomes a real high-speed system?

That question became real in my professional work at Siliconova, where I contributed to a 6G-oriented satellite and RF research platform designed for remote deployments where internet connectivity may be limited or unavailable. The system captures wideband RF data from FPGA-based SDRs, moves it through a 100 Gbps-class DPDK data path, and records the data locally so researchers can later replay and analyze large datasets for signal studies and machine-learning-based anomaly-detection research.

System at a glance

End-to-end satellite data acquisition and telemetry architecture: remote SDR capture → high-speed DPDK processing → telemetry extraction → gRPC services → Prometheus and Grafana monitoring.

01

How does the signal enter the system?

Satellite RF signals are acquired through FPGA-based SDR front ends, where raw I/Q samples are framed, timestamped, sequence-tracked, and transmitted over high-speed Ethernet to the capture server.

Satellite → SDR / FPGA → I/Q Data → Ethernet → Capture Server
02

How do you handle that much data reliably?

At the server, a DPDK-based user-space data plane uses kernel bypass, HugePages, and dedicated processing paths to handle high-rate traffic efficiently. Telemetry is separated from the critical fast path through shared memory and lightweight synchronization so monitoring does not interrupt packet processing.

100 Gbps Ingress → DPDK → Local Recording
03

How do you know the captured data is trustworthy?

Captured datasets are replayed through an isolated validation path and checked for packet continuity, sequence integrity, anomalies, and runtime performance.

The result is a platform that allows researchers to capture massive satellite datasets during short remote observation windows, store them locally, and later use them for deeper signal analysis and anomaly-detection research.
