About

My Research Journey

From wireless research to intelligent communication systems

Undergraduate Thesis

Understanding the wireless channel

My journey began with undergraduate research on wireless fading and physical-layer security. I studied a dual-hop wireless system with randomly distributed eavesdroppers using the generalized α−µ fading model.

The study showed that simultaneous eavesdropping across both hops creates the most critical secrecy condition, while stronger legitimate channels and receive diversity improve secure communication.

Professional Experience

My professional engineering experience contributed to work related to the TruSystems Platform for Research & Prototyping by XRComm . Product and platform credit belongs to XRComm.

From channel theory to real RF systems

At Siliconova, I moved from studying wireless channels theoretically to working with real high-speed RF systems. I contributed to satellite data acquisition using FPGA-based SDRs and a 100 Gbps-class DPDK data path for capturing large RF datasets in remote environments.

This experience made me interested not only in how a signal behaves mathematically, but also in how real RF data can be captured, preserved, and analyzed.

VLSI & Future Research

Going deeper into the hardware and the signal

Working closely with FPGA data paths developed my interest in VLSI, RTL design, RISC-V, and hardware verification. It also raised a new question for me: how can the captured RF data be understood more intelligently?

My future research interest is to combine wireless channel analysis, real RF measurements, AI-based anomaly detection, and FPGA hardware acceleration.

I want to study how AI can detect signal anomalies and changing fading conditions from real RF measurements, while FPGA acceleration enables faster processing closer to the hardware.

I aim to pursue an M.Sc. and eventually a Ph.D. to develop this research direction more deeply.
